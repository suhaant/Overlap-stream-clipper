The signal

It isn't the words. It's that everyone types the same word at once.

The obvious build matches chat against a lexicon of KEKW, LOL, POG. Measured against 6,095 messages of real GTA 6 chat, that ranked pure conversation above the best moment in the run.

The best detection of the run scored 0.01 on vocabulary. The worst false positive scored 0.50, because one person wrote "@M0hamdQ LOL". Convergence separates them cleanly, and because it never reads the words themselves it works unchanged on Spanish chat and on a game whose slang does not exist yet. No per-title tuning.

The gap

Two sentences from overlap.ai, four days apart.

Connect your Twitch account and have your AI agent watch every second of your stream, read every chat message, and generate clips in real time. No more waiting until the stream ends. Overlap, announcing Twitch support
Stream to Twitch or YouTube and keep the VOD. Overlap, "How to Clip GTA 6" — step one, published 18 Nov, the day before launch

The flagship guide for the biggest game launch in history still assumes the VOD workflow. During launch week, when Overlap's own post says attention is highest, a VOD pipeline posts hours late. This is the thing that closes that gap.

Why it is a margin problem

Selection precision is gross margin, not polish.

Overlap bills $0.50–$2.50 CPM on delivered views and absorbs the downside: "nothing charged for reach that did not land." Every clip that flops costs render time and earns zero. For most clipping tools, picking badly is a user complaint. Here it is on the P&L. A detector that fires on conversation half the time makes that worse; one that doesn't is a direct margin improvement.

What it does not do yet

It knows chat erupted. It does not know why.

Captions still come from a small set of phrases keyed to moment type. The top detection in the run — 86 people typing "heist" — is correctly labelled unclear, because detection is vocabulary-independent but naming a moment is not. Closing that needs a vision or LLM pass over the clip window. It is the most interesting thing left to build.

Rights are the other open edge. Overlap's own guide is blunt that monetized GTA content is "tolerated, not licensed," and flags story spoilers and in-game radio as the main takedown risks. A spoiler gate and music detection belong in this pipeline before it points at a paying campaign.
